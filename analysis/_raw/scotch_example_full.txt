1. Introduction
Scotch Partners are delighted to have been invited by Harry Keen of LGL to provide a fee
and scope for the design of the 83 New Kent Road redevelopment. This is a great project
and we are delighted to have been involved during the acquisition stage of the scheme and
to provide recommendations on how to develop the site and achieve EPC B.
We understand that the scheme is close to exchange/ completion and LGL are keen to
receive proposals so that the design team can hit the ground running. Scotch partners will
propose to keep the same team on the project that undertook the acquisition. To
summarise the scheme entails the following:-
• The site is currently a student residential for London South Bank University
• The proposal is to convert the various blocks into 93 No. separate studios each with
separate kitchen and shower/ WC area
• It is anticipated that the proposal will not require a planning application ( only minor
amendment)
• The development is to be treated as ‘non domestic’ for energy/ EPC purposes.
• High level program includes for Stage 2 by mid may with the tender process
commencing in July 2025.
o Stage 3+ issued by Mid October
o Final completion and handover by mid June 2027
• Fees have been provided for MEP, Sustainability Energy and Acoustics

2. Scotch Partners Introduction
Scotch have a proven track record working with Workplace and Science and Technology
clients and are currently working a number of similar residential/ co- living schemes. Scotch
are a building design practice providing mechanical, electrical & public health engineering,
sustainability, energy and planning (SEP) and acoustic consultancy services.
The culture of the practice is founded upon respect for people and trust. We are committed
to creating a great and inclusive working environment where the best minds in the industry
will flourish.
This is supported by our high retention rate which was c97% for recently completed FY 24.
Since its inception, Scotch has organically grown being careful to employ people that
believe in our shared values of quality, collaboration, and trust.
With a team of circa 65 people, our size and ownership enable us to have total control over
workload and resource, working on small, medium, and large-scale projects. Most of our
project work is through repeat business or recommendations from people we work with.
This is testament to the team and the work we produce which is founded on quality and
attention to detail.
We work on a wide variety of projects in relation to the type and scale of this project, with
a high proportion of our work within the workplace and science and technology sectors.
Management Structure
Scotch operates as one business unit / team reflected by the flat and simple management
structure. There are 6 partners, with John Steele recently joining as a Partner, that are all
involved and focused on project delivery. Reporting into the Partners and helping deliver
the projects are the Technical Directors, which includes a Business Operations Director.
Our flat company structure means that we do not work in isolated, internal teams thereby
allowing us flexibility to allocate staff as required. This also allows for sharing of knowledge
across the business. As the company is wholly owned by the Partners, we have complete
control over our operation and resource.

3. Team and Proposed Roles
3.1 Organogram
We propose to use the same team members that supported the acquisition stage of the
project as this team is known to LGL. The team are all based in our London office in
Farringdon.
In order to establish clear lines of communication between the client and project team we
have proposed to structure ourselves in a way to delineate reporting and communication
with wider stakeholders.
We propose to create a core leadership team with John Steele the overall point of contact
for day to day matters. John will be supported by Mark Bacon (leading on utilities and the
electrical Peer Review) and Kirsten Elder and Jason Clouston, should we also be appointed
for sustainability and acoustics. This provides a clear leadership structure for reporting,
providing feedback and will allow existing relationships to develop further with the client
team.
We acknowledge that a similar approach is needed to communicate with the wider team so
we propose to use Raphael Boothman as the Scotch MEP co-ordinator. Raphael’s role will
be to liaise with the wider design team to ensure that information flows smoothly and co-
ordination matters are dealt with swiftly and pro-actively.
